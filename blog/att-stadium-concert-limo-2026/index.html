Concert Traffic vs. Football Traffic — Why It's Different

Fans who've only done Cowboys games are often caught off guard by concert nights. The differences matter:

  • Everyone arrives at once. Football fans trickle in over the four hours lots are open for tailgating; concert crowds pack the roads in a tight window before showtime, so Collins Street and Randol Mill Road spike harder and later.
  • The post-show exit is brutal. There's no slow trickle out like a football crowd — 70,000 people leave in the same 30 minutes, and self-parkers can sit an hour-plus.
  • Weeknight shows hit rush hour. A Thursday show like Karol G (Oct 15) or the mid-week R&B Tour dates layers concert traffic on top of commuter traffic.

A chauffeur solves all three: staged at your pickup point before the encore ends, routing around the worst corridors, and no one in your group stuck as designated driver. For the full route breakdown, see our guide on getting to AT&T Stadium in 2026 — the same drop-off logic applies to concerts.

There's also a cost angle fans miss. After a stadium concert, tens of thousands of people open the same rideshare app at the same minute, and prices spike into deep surge territory — sometimes two or three times the normal fare, if a car shows up at all. Flat-rate limo pricing doesn't move. The quote you booked weeks ago is the quote you pay at 11pm when the encore ends, which for a group splitting the cost often comes out cheaper than surge rideshares plus what you'd have spent on a $50 parking lot.

Timing a Concert Night: When to Leave

Concert doors and set times run later than a football kickoff, so plan the day differently. Because the whole crowd funnels in over a tight window, leave earlier than feels necessary — being dropped 90 minutes before the headliner beats sitting on Collins Street watching the opener from your car. Have your chauffeur build in a pre-show dinner stop in the Arlington Entertainment District so the early arrival is a feature, not dead time. Then agree on a firm post-show pickup point before you go in; after the encore, texting a driver in a 70,000-person crowd is far easier when you both already know the spot.
